Front Raise

strengthbeginnerisolationoverheadbilateral

The front raise is a shoulder isolation exercise targeting the anterior (front) head of the deltoid through shoulder flexion. It can be performed with dumbbells, a barbell, or a plate. It is typically used as an accessory exercise to complement compound overhead pressing movements.

Primary muscles

deltoid anterior

Secondary muscles

deltoid lateral
pectoralis major clavicular

Stabilizers

trapezius upper

Equipment

Dumbbell (Fixed)

Starting position

  • Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Hold a dumbbell in each hand in front of the thighs with palms facing back (pronated) or neutral.

Setup

  • Use light to moderate weight. The anterior deltoid is frequently overtrained from pressing movements, so this is primarily a finishing or accessory exercise.

Steps

  1. With a slight elbow bend, raise both dumbbells in front of you to approximately shoulder height.
  2. Pause briefly at the top, then lower under control to the starting position.

Coaching cues

  • Keep your torso still — only your arms should move
  • Maintain a slight elbow bend throughout — do not lock out

Breathing

  • Exhale on the raise, inhale on the descent.

Range of motion

  • Raise to shoulder height. Going higher primarily increases trap involvement.

Tempo

  • 2-1-3-0

Common mistakes

  • Swinging the torso to generate momentum
  • Raising the dumbbells above shoulder height unnecessarily

Safety

  • The anterior deltoid is typically already well-trained from pressing movements — program conservatively to avoid overuse

Training

  • hypertrophy: 12–20 reps
  • hypertrophy: 2–4 sets
