Lying Leg Curl

strengthbeginnerisolationpullbilateral

The lying leg curl is performed face-down on a machine, curling the pad from full knee extension to maximum knee flexion against a resistance arm. As an isolation exercise for the hamstrings, it places the hip in extension, which allows the biceps femoris long head to reach a shortened position and trains the hamstrings through the knee flexion function that many compound exercises underemphasize.

Equipment

Lying Leg Curl Machine

Starting position

  • Lie face down on the leg curl machine. Position the pad just above the heel of the ankle (on the lower calf). Grip the handles and ensure the knees are just at or slightly off the edge of the pad.

Setup

  • Adjust the machine so the pivot point aligns with the knee joint. The pad should contact just above the heel cord. Set a resistance appropriate for controlled full-range movement.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Lie prone, grip the handles, and position the pad on the lower calf near the ankle.
  2. Step 2: Curl the pad upward by flexing the knees as far as the machine allows — ideally to 120–140° of knee flexion.
  3. Step 3: Hold briefly at the top with the hamstrings fully contracted.
  4. Step 4: Lower the weight under control back to full knee extension — do not let the weight stack crash.
  5. Step 5: Repeat for the prescribed number of reps.

Coaching cues

  • Don't let the hips leave the pad — keep them pressed down
  • Control the weight all the way down — no crashing
  • Squeeze the hamstrings at the top of each rep

Breathing

  • Exhale as you curl up. Inhale as you lower.

Range of motion

  • Full extension at the bottom to full available flexion at the top. The hamstrings should be under load through the entire range.

Tempo

  • 2-1-3-0 — a slow eccentric (3 seconds) maximizes time under tension

Common mistakes

  • Using momentum — the hips should not rise off the pad during the curl
  • Using a range that stops short of full extension, reducing the hamstring stretch
  • Letting the weight stack crash at the bottom, which loses the eccentric stimulus

Safety

  • If hip flexors are tight, the hip will flex during the curl — this reduces stimulus and can cause low back discomfort
  • Avoid this machine if you have posterior knee issues — discuss with a clinician

Training

  • strength: 6–10 reps
  • hypertrophy: 10–20 reps
  • endurance: 15–25 reps
  • strength: 3–4 sets
  • hypertrophy: 3–4 sets
  • endurance: 2–3 sets
