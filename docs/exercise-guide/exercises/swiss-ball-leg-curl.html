Swiss Ball Leg Curl

strengthintermediatecompoundhingebilateral

The Swiss ball leg curl is a bodyweight hamstring exercise performed with the heels resting on a stability ball. The lifter begins in a supine bridge position, then curls the ball toward the hips using knee flexion while maintaining a hip bridge. The instability of the ball increases glute and core activation compared to a lying machine curl. It is an accessible exercise requiring no gym equipment beyond a stability ball.

Primary muscles

biceps femoris long head
semitendinosus

Secondary muscles

gluteus maximus

Stabilizers

transverse abdominis
gluteus medius

Equipment

Swiss Ball (Stability Ball / Exercise Ball)

Starting position

  • Lie on your back. Place both heels on a Swiss ball with legs extended. Arms by sides or slightly out for stability.

Setup

  • Inflate the Swiss ball to firm. Place it on a non-slip surface. Lie supine with heels on the ball.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Drive the hips up into a bridge position, body forming a straight line from shoulders to heels.
  2. Step 2: While maintaining the hip bridge, curl the ball toward your hips by bending the knees.
  3. Step 3: Pull the ball until the knees are at approximately 90 degrees.
  4. Step 4: Extend the knees to roll the ball back out, maintaining the hip bridge throughout.

Coaching cues

  • Hips stay high throughout
  • Curl the ball with your heels
  • Control the roll back out

Breathing

  • Exhale on the curl, inhale on the extension.

Range of motion

  • Full leg extension at the start, curl to 90 degrees or more of knee flexion.

Tempo

  • 2-1-3-0

Common mistakes

  • Hips dropping during the curl, losing the bridge position
  • Ball rolling to one side due to uneven foot pressure
  • Rushing the eccentric extension

Safety

  • Ensure the ball is on a non-slip surface
  • Keep the core braced throughout to protect the lumbar spine

Training

  • strength: 8–12 reps
  • hypertrophy: 12–20 reps
  • strength: 3–4 sets
  • hypertrophy: 3–4 sets
