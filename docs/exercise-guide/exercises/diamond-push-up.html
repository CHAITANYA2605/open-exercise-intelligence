Diamond Push-Up

strengthintermediatecalisthenicspushbilateral

The diamond push-up is a push-up variation where the thumbs and index fingers touch to form a diamond or triangle shape beneath the chest. This narrow hand position dramatically increases triceps brachii activation while the pectoralis major acts as a secondary mover. It is one of the most effective bodyweight exercises for tricep development.

Primary muscles

triceps lateral head
triceps medial head
triceps long head

Secondary muscles

pectoralis major sternal
deltoid anterior

Stabilizers

transverse abdominis
pectoralis minor

Equipment

No Equipment (Bodyweight)

Starting position

  • Begin in a standard push-up position, then bring both hands together under the sternum with the thumbs and forefingers touching to form a diamond shape. Arms should be fully extended.

Setup

  • No equipment needed. Find a flat, stable surface for the hands.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Position hands together on the floor below the chest with thumbs and index fingers touching to form a diamond shape.
  2. Step 2: Extend arms fully; keep the body in a rigid plank from head to heels.
  3. Step 3: Inhale and lower the chest toward the diamond by bending the elbows, which will move outward slightly to either side.
  4. Step 4: Lower until the chest nearly touches the hands, pause briefly, then exhale and press back to the starting position.

Coaching cues

  • Think 'elbows back' as you descend to maximize tricep engagement
  • Keep the torso rigid — imagine a straight rod from crown to heel

Breathing

  • Inhale on the descent; exhale on the press.

Range of motion

  • Lower the chest to touch or nearly touch the hands, then press fully back to extension.

Tempo

  • 3-1-1-0

Common mistakes

  • Flaring the elbows wide, which shifts load away from the triceps
  • Letting the hips drop or rise out of a neutral plank position

Safety

  • If wrist discomfort occurs, slightly rotate hands outward a few degrees
  • Reduce range of motion if shoulder impingement is felt

Training

  • strength: 5–10 reps
  • hypertrophy: 10–20 reps
  • endurance: 15–30 reps
  • strength: 3–4 sets
  • hypertrophy: 3–4 sets
  • endurance: 2–3 sets
