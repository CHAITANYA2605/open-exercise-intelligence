Z-Press with Dumbbells (Neutral Grip)

strengthintermediatecompoundpushbilateral

The dumbbell neutral-grip Z-press is performed seated on the floor with legs extended, pressing dumbbells overhead with palms facing each other. The floor-seated position eliminates leg drive and forces upright torso posture via hamstring and hip flexor flexibility demands. The neutral grip is more comfortable for many trainees than the standard pronated Z-press grip, reducing wrist and elbow stress while maintaining the same shoulder loading pattern.

Primary muscles

deltoid anterior
deltoid lateral

Secondary muscles

triceps brachii
erector spinae

Stabilizers

transverse abdominis

Equipment

Standard Barbell

Starting position

  • Sit on the floor with legs fully extended straight in front. Hold dumbbells at shoulder height with a neutral (hammer) grip. Sit upright — torso perpendicular to the floor.

Setup

  • If hamstring tightness prevents an upright torso, perform with a slight back support initially. Moderate weight — the floor position eliminates leg drive entirely.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Begin seated upright on the floor, dumbbells at shoulder height neutral grip.
  2. Step 2: Press both dumbbells overhead, maintaining neutral wrist position.
  3. Step 3: At full lockout, shrug the traps slightly into the top position.
  4. Step 4: Lower under control back to shoulder level.
  5. Step 5: Maintain upright torso throughout — no leaning back.

Coaching cues

  • Sit tall — no lean
  • Legs flat and relaxed
  • Drive the weights straight overhead

Breathing

  • Exhale pressing. Inhale on the descent.

Range of motion

  • Shoulder height to full overhead lockout.

Tempo

  • 2-1-2-0

Common mistakes

  • Leaning back as the press becomes difficult — the upright position is non-negotiable
  • Feet rotating or legs lifting — legs stay flat on the floor

Safety

  • Adequate hamstring flexibility required — do not force a rounded-back position
  • Build up load slowly — no leg drive means lower absolute loads
