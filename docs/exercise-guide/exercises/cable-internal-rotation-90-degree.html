Cable Internal Rotation at 90 Degrees

rehabilitationbeginnerisolationrotationunilateral

Cable internal rotation at 90 degrees abduction trains the subscapularis — the primary internal rotator and the largest rotator cuff muscle — with the shoulder abducted to 90 degrees. This angle tests internal rotation strength in the most functionally loaded position for overhead athletes, and weak internal rotation at 90 degrees is associated with increased injury risk in throwers.

Primary muscles

deltoid anterior

Secondary muscles

deltoid lateral

Equipment

Cable Machine (High Pulley)

Starting position

  • Set a cable pulley above shoulder height. Stand side-on with the working shoulder abducted to 90 degrees, elbow bent to 90 degrees, forearm pointing toward the ceiling. Grip the cable handle.

Setup

  • The cable should pull from above when the forearm is in the starting overhead position. Use light weight.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Begin with the shoulder at 90 degrees abduction, forearm pointing to ceiling, elbow at 90 degrees.
  2. Step 2: Keeping the elbow fixed at shoulder height, rotate the forearm downward.
  3. Step 3: Continue until the forearm is pointing toward the floor.
  4. Step 4: Hold 1 second.
  5. Step 5: Rotate back to the starting position.

Coaching cues

  • Elbow is the pivot point — it does not move
  • Forearm rotates down — not the entire arm
  • Light and controlled

Breathing

  • Exhale rotating down. Inhale returning.

Range of motion

  • Forearm from ceiling to floor — approximately 180 degrees.

Tempo

  • 2-1-2-0

Common mistakes

  • Elbow dropping below shoulder level
  • Upper arm adducting (moving toward the body) instead of only the forearm rotating

Safety

  • Very light loads — subscapularis isolation is performed with low resistance
  • Stop if sharp anterior shoulder pain occurs

Training

  • rehabilitation: 12–20 reps
  • rehabilitation: 2–3 sets
