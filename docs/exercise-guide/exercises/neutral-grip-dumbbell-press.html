Neutral-Grip Dumbbell Bench Press

strengthbeginnercompoundpushbilateral

The Neutral-Grip Dumbbell Bench Press is performed with the palms facing each other (thumbs pointing toward the ceiling) throughout the movement. This grip reduces external rotation demand on the shoulder joint compared to a pronated grip, making it a preferable option for individuals with shoulder impingement, AC joint issues, or rotator cuff concerns while maintaining effective pectoral and triceps training.

Primary muscles

Equipment

Dumbbell (Fixed)
Flat Bench

Starting position

  • Lie flat on a bench holding a dumbbell in each hand with palms facing each other. Dumbbells positioned at chest level, elbows bent at approximately 90 degrees.

Setup

  • No special setup required. The neutral grip is natural with dumbbells — simply orient the grip so thumbs point upward rather than forward.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Press the dumbbells upward from the chest, maintaining the neutral grip throughout.
  2. Step 2: Extend the elbows to full lockout.
  3. Step 3: Lower back to the starting position, keeping palms facing each other.
  4. Step 4: Repeat for the prescribed reps.

Coaching cues

  • Palms face each other — keep them facing each other at all times
  • Elbows track toward the feet, not flaring to the sides

Breathing

  • Inhale before lowering. Exhale as you press up.

Range of motion

  • Full elbow extension at top; elbows descend to 90 degrees or slightly below at bottom.

Tempo

  • 3-1-1-0

Common mistakes

  • Rotating the grip to pronated mid-set, losing the joint-friendly benefit
  • Allowing the elbows to flare wide

Safety

  • This is recommended for individuals with anterior shoulder pain — do not force a pronated grip if pain is present
  • Consult a healthcare provider if shoulder pain persists

Training

  • strength: 5–8 reps
  • hypertrophy: 8–15 reps
  • strength: 3–5 sets
  • hypertrophy: 3–4 sets
