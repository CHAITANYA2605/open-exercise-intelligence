Exercise guide / Barbell Row

Barbell Row

strengthintermediatecompoundpullbilateral

The barbell row is a horizontal pulling exercise performed in a hinged position with a barbell. It trains the latissimus dorsi, rhomboids, and middle trapezius as primary movers and places significant isometric demand on the erector spinae and hamstrings to maintain the hip-hinge position. It is one of the most effective exercises for overall back thickness and strength.

Equipment

Standard Barbell

Optional

Lifting Straps

Starting position

  • Stand with feet shoulder-width apart over the barbell. Hinge forward at the hips until your torso is roughly parallel to the floor, with a slight knee bend. Grip the bar just outside shoulder width with an overhand or underhand grip.

Setup

  • Set up like the start of a deadlift but with a more pronounced hip hinge to get the torso near-parallel to the floor. Maintain a neutral spine. Brace the core and engage the lats before each rep.

Steps

  1. With a neutral spine and braced core, row the barbell toward your lower rib cage.
  2. Lead with the elbows and squeeze the shoulder blades together at the top of the movement.
  3. Lower the bar under control back toward the floor, maintaining the hinge position throughout.

Coaching cues

  • Row the bar into your hip pockets
  • Keep your chest up and your spine long throughout every rep

Breathing

  • Exhale as you row the bar up. Inhale as you lower it.

Range of motion

  • Full extension of the arms at the bottom, bar touches or nearly touches the lower ribs at the top.

Tempo

  • 2-1-1-0

Common mistakes

  • Allowing the lower back to round under load
  • Using body English and hip drive to jerk the bar up instead of pulling with the back
  • Pulling the bar to the waist rather than the lower ribs, reducing lat involvement

Safety

  • Never compromise spinal position for more weight
  • If unable to maintain a hip-hinge position, reduce load

Training

  • strength: 3–6 reps
  • hypertrophy: 6–12 reps
  • endurance: 12–15 reps
  • strength: 3–5 sets
  • hypertrophy: 3–5 sets
  • endurance: 2–4 sets
