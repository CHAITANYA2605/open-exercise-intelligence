High Push-Up Position Plank

strengthbeginnercompoundplankbilateral

The high push-up position plank is a foundational isometric core stability exercise in which the body is held in a rigid, straight line from head to heel while supported on the hands and toes. Compared to the forearm plank, the extended-arm position places greater demand on the shoulder girdle stabilizers and serratus anterior, while the core musculature functions identically to maintain spinal stiffness. It is a prerequisite position for push-up and many other calisthenic progressions and should be mastered before advancing to dynamic variations.

Equipment

No Equipment (Bodyweight)

Starting position

  • Place the hands on the floor directly under the shoulders. Extend the legs behind, toes on the floor. The body forms a straight line from head to heel.

Setup

  • No equipment needed. Use a mat for wrist comfort on hard surfaces.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Position hands under shoulders, fingers spread for grip.
  2. Step 2: Drive the floor away — imagine pushing the ground down. Protract the scapulae slightly.
  3. Step 3: Squeeze the glutes and brace the core. Lock out the knees.
  4. Step 4: Keep the body in a straight line — no hip hiking, sagging, or piking.
  5. Step 5: Hold for the target duration, maintaining perfect alignment throughout.

Coaching cues

  • Push the floor away
  • Squeeze the glutes — the whole posterior chain is a unit
  • Long spine — from crown to heel

Breathing

  • Breathe continuously with shallow breaths. Do not hold the breath.

Range of motion

  • Isometric hold. Perfect alignment must be maintained for the full duration.

Tempo

  • Hold for target time: beginners 20–30 s; intermediate 45–60 s; advanced 60–90 s+.

Common mistakes

  • Hips piking up — this reduces core demand and places it on the shoulders
  • Hips sagging — this compresses the lumbar spine under load

Safety

  • Avoid if wrist pain is present — substitute forearm plank

Training

  • endurance: 1–3 reps
  • endurance: 3–5 sets
