Underhand T-Bar Row

strengthintermediatecompoundpullbilateral

The underhand T-bar row uses a supinated (palms-up) grip on the T-bar, allowing the elbows to track close to the body and placing the biceps in a mechanically advantageous position. The supinated grip enables a fuller range of motion and facilitates lower lat engagement at the bottom of the pull, making it a popular choice for developing lower lat thickness.

Primary muscles

latissimus dorsi

Secondary muscles

biceps brachii
rhomboid major

Stabilizers

erector spinae

Equipment

T-Bar Row

Starting position

  • Straddle the T-bar machine. Turn the hands supinated (palms up) on the handles or bar. Hinge to 45-60 degrees, arms extended.

Setup

  • Some T-bar machines do not accommodate a supinated grip — in that case, use a straight bar end on a landmine setup where the hands can grip palm-up.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Begin with arms extended, palms facing up.
  2. Step 2: Initiate the row by driving the elbows back alongside the torso.
  3. Step 3: Pull until the elbows pass torso level.
  4. Step 4: Squeeze at the top, feeling the lower lats contract.
  5. Step 5: Lower under control to full arm extension.

Coaching cues

  • Palms face up throughout
  • Elbows track alongside the ribs
  • Feel the stretch under the arms at the bottom

Breathing

  • Exhale on the row. Inhale on the descent.

Range of motion

  • Full arm extension to full lat contraction.

Tempo

  • 2-1-2-0

Common mistakes

  • Supinating the wrists so far it becomes uncomfortable — find a comfortable angle
  • Bicep-dominant pulling — think lat stretch and contraction, not curl

Safety

  • The supinated wrist position can stress the wrist joint — progress load conservatively
  • Avoid this variation with wrist injuries

Training

  • hypertrophy: 8–15 reps
  • hypertrophy: 3–4 sets
