Cable Internal Rotation (Low Pulley)

rehabilitationbeginnerisolationrotationunilateral

Cable internal rotation from a low pulley trains the subscapularis — the primary internal rotator and largest rotator cuff muscle — with the shoulder adducted at the side and elbow bent to 90 degrees. This is the most commonly prescribed rotator cuff strengthening exercise in rehabilitation settings and is performed with extremely light loads for high repetitions.

Primary muscles

deltoid anterior

Secondary muscles

None listed

Equipment

Cable Machine (High Pulley)

Starting position

  • Stand with one side facing the cable machine. Set the pulley at waist height. Elbow of the working arm bent to 90 degrees, upper arm held against the side, forearm pointing away from the cable.

Setup

  • Place a rolled towel between the working-side elbow and the torso to maintain consistent positioning. Use very light weight.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Begin with elbow at 90 degrees, forearm pointing away from the cable.
  2. Step 2: Keeping the upper arm fixed against the side, rotate the forearm inward (toward the abdomen).
  3. Step 3: Continue until the forearm is parallel to the floor pointing toward the abdomen.
  4. Step 4: Hold 1 second.
  5. Step 5: Return to starting position.

Coaching cues

  • Upper arm stays glued to the side
  • Rotate only the forearm
  • Towel under elbow helps maintain position

Breathing

  • Exhale rotating in. Inhale returning.

Range of motion

  • Forearm from pointing away from cable to pointing across the abdomen.

Tempo

  • 2-1-2-0

Common mistakes

  • Upper arm moving away from the torso — it must stay fixed
  • Using too much load — subscapularis work requires light resistance

Safety

  • Light load only — 2-5 kg is typical for this exercise
  • Appropriate for everyone as a shoulder health exercise

Training

  • rehabilitation: 15–25 reps
  • rehabilitation: 2–3 sets
