Trap Bar Romanian Deadlift

strengthintermediatecompoundhingebilateral

A Romanian deadlift performed with a trap bar (hex bar). The trap bars neutral grip and symmetrical loading reduces torque on the lumbar spine compared to a standard barbell RDL, while still training the hip hinge pattern with an emphasis on glute and hamstring development through a large range of eccentric loading.

Primary muscles

gluteus maximus
biceps femoris long head
semitendinosus

Secondary muscles

erector spinae
adductor magnus
semimembranosus

Stabilizers

transverse abdominis
flexor carpi ulnaris

Equipment

Trap Bar (Hex Bar)

Starting position

  • Stand inside the trap bar with feet hip-width apart, gripping the handles. Bar should be loaded on the floor or start from the top position.

Setup

  • Load the trap bar. Stand inside it, grip the handles, and stand upright with hips fully extended as the starting position.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Keeping the knees soft and spine neutral, hinge at the hips and push them back.
  2. Step 2: Lower the bar along the sides of the thighs, feeling a progressive stretch in the hamstrings.
  3. Step 3: Once a strong hamstring stretch is felt (typically mid-shin), drive the hips forward.
  4. Step 4: Extend the hips fully at the top, squeezing the glutes, and return to the starting position.

Coaching cues

  • Push the hips back, not the chest down
  • Feel the hamstring stretch at the bottom

Breathing

  • Inhale and brace before hinging. Exhale on the drive to standing.

Range of motion

  • Lower until a strong hamstring stretch is felt. Full hip extension at the top.

Tempo

  • 3-0-2-0

Common mistakes

  • Rounding the lower back at the bottom of the movement
  • Bending the knees excessively, turning it into a deadlift

Safety

  • Keep the bar close to your body throughout the movement
  • Do not hyperextend the lower back at the top

Training

  • strength: 3–8 reps
  • hypertrophy: 8–15 reps
  • strength: 3–5 sets
  • hypertrophy: 3–4 sets
