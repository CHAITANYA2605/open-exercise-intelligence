Exercise guide / Dead Bug

Dead Bug

rehabilitationbeginnercalisthenicsanti-rotationalternating

The dead bug is an anti-extension core stability exercise performed supine. By alternately extending opposite arm and leg while maintaining a stable spine, it challenges the deep core musculature to resist lumbar extension. It is widely used in rehabilitation and athletic training for spinal stability and motor control development.

Primary muscles

transverse abdominis

Secondary muscles

rectus abdominis
internal oblique

Stabilizers

erector spinae

Equipment

No Equipment (Bodyweight)

Starting position

  • Lie on your back with arms extended toward the ceiling and knees bent at 90 degrees (tabletop position). Lower back is pressed flat into the floor.

Setup

  • Press the entire lower back into the floor before starting. This position must be maintained throughout each rep.

Steps

  1. Simultaneously extend the right arm backward toward the floor and extend the left leg forward toward the floor.
  2. Lower both limbs as far as possible while maintaining the lower back pressed flat against the floor.
  3. Return to the starting position and repeat on the opposite side.

Coaching cues

  • Push your lower back into the floor like you are pressing it through the earth
  • Move slowly — control every inch of the limb extension

Breathing

  • Exhale as you extend the limbs. Inhale as you return.

Range of motion

  • Extend as far as possible while the lower back remains flat on the floor.

Tempo

  • 3-0-3-0

Common mistakes

  • Allowing the lower back to lift off the floor during the limb extension
  • Moving too fast — control is the purpose of this exercise
  • Extending same-side arm and leg instead of opposite

Safety

  • This is a low-risk exercise appropriate for most populations including those with lower back issues

Training

  • endurance: 8–16 reps
  • endurance: 2–4 sets
