Narrow-Grip Decline Barbell Bench Press

strengthintermediatecompoundpushbilateral

A decline bench press variation using a close, narrow grip that heavily shifts the emphasis from the pectorals to the triceps. The decline angle combined with the narrow grip maximizes triceps long head activation while still providing meaningful lower pectoralis major loading. Favored by powerlifters for triceps development and by bodybuilders targeting the sternal pec fibers with a triceps-intensive pressing movement.

Primary muscles

triceps long head
triceps lateral head

Secondary muscles

pectoralis major sternal
deltoid anterior

Stabilizers

pectoralis minor

Equipment

Standard Barbell
Adjustable Bench (Incline / Decline)
Power Rack (Squat Cage)

Starting position

  • Lie on a decline bench (15–30 degrees decline) with feet secured. Grip the barbell with hands roughly shoulder-width apart or slightly narrower. Bar above the lower chest.

Setup

  • Set the decline bench at 15–30 degrees. Ensure the rack height is appropriate. Secure feet. The grip should be shoulder-width — not so narrow that it stresses the wrists.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Unrack the bar and position it above the lower chest, arms extended.
  2. Step 2: Lower the bar to the lower chest keeping elbows relatively tucked (not flared) due to the narrow grip.
  3. Step 3: Touch the lower chest and immediately press back up, extending the elbows forcefully.
  4. Step 4: Fully lock out the elbows at the top. The triceps should be under significant tension at lockout.

Coaching cues

  • Tuck the elbows to work the triceps — flaring negates the grip advantage
  • Squeeze hard at lockout — the decline + narrow grip puts peak triceps tension at the top

Breathing

  • Inhale before descent, exhale forcefully on the press.

Range of motion

  • Full range from lower chest to full elbow lockout. The narrow grip actually lengthens the range of motion at the elbow.

Tempo

  • 3-1-2-1

Common mistakes

  • Grip too narrow — hands closer than shoulder width causes excessive wrist stress
  • Letting elbows flare wide, reducing triceps contribution
  • Feet unsecured on the decline bench

Safety

  • Secure feet firmly on the decline; feet unsecured is a safety risk
  • Use a spotter for heavier sets
  • Avoid grips inside shoulder-width — wrist pain is a common result

Training

  • strength: 3–6 reps
  • hypertrophy: 6–10 reps
  • strength: 4–5 sets
  • hypertrophy: 3–4 sets
