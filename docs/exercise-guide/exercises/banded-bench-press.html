Banded Resistance Barbell Bench Press

strengthadvancedcompoundpushbilateral

The Banded Bench Press attaches resistance bands to the barbell and anchors them below the bench, creating accommodating resistance that increases as the bar approaches lockout. The bands also create an overspeed eccentric on the way down, demanding greater eccentric control. This tool trains explosive pressing intent and lockout strength simultaneously.

Primary muscles

pectoralis major sternal
pectoralis major clavicular

Secondary muscles

deltoid anterior
triceps lateral head
triceps medial head

Stabilizers

subscapularis
pectoralis minor

Equipment

Standard Barbell
Power Rack (Squat Cage)
Resistance Band (Loop / Mini Band)

Starting position

  • Loop bands around the barbell sleeves and anchor them under the bench feet or to the base of a power rack. Lie on the bench and grip the bar as for a standard bench press.

Setup

  • Secure bands symmetrically on both sides with equal tension. Reduce barbell plate load to account for band tension at lockout. The total load at lockout equals barbell weight plus band tension.

Steps

  1. Step 1: Unrack the bar; feel the band tension pulling the bar downward.
  2. Step 2: Lower under full control — the bands create an overspeed eccentric pull.
  3. Step 3: Touch the chest and immediately press with maximal intent.
  4. Step 4: Drive to lockout against the increasing band resistance.

Coaching cues

  • Fight the bands down and explode up
  • Press as fast as possible — your intent drives the adaptation

Breathing

  • Inhale and brace before the eccentric. Exhale through lockout.

Range of motion

  • Full ROM — chest to lockout. Same as standard bench press.

Tempo

  • 3-0-X-0

Common mistakes

  • Using bands that are too heavy, turning the eccentric into an uncontrollable drop
  • Overloading the barbell — total load at lockout should not exceed your true max

Safety

  • Bands can slip if not anchored correctly — double-check anchor points
  • Always use a power rack with safety bars

Training

  • strength: 1–5 reps
  • power: 2–5 reps
  • strength: 3–6 sets
  • power: 4–8 sets
  • Rest: {'strength': 240, 'power': 180}
